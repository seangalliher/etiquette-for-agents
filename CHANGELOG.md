# Changelog

## 0.1 (2026-11-16)

First edition.

- `CORE.md`: eleven core principles and the Never list, which sits below law and safety and above organization policy.
- `book/`: six parts. Every chapter follows the same template (Principle, Why it matters, Do, Don't, Hard calls) and traces to the core principles. Includes the glossary and quick reference.
- `evals/`: 25 public scenarios (27 counting variants) covering each core principle and the main Never items, a runner that executes them with simulated tools and grades them, and the raw results of every public run. Published results also use private held-out sets, including a sealed set run once after the text was final.
- [Results](https://www.bookforagents.com/results.html) for this edition: eight models from four vendors, with and without the book, including where it made no difference and where it made things worse.
- Website at [www.bookforagents.com](https://www.bookforagents.com), with plain-text versions for agents (`core.md`, `llms.txt`, `llms-full.txt`) and the chapters packaged as an Agent Skill (`etiquette-for-agents.zip`).
- Changes made during review: principle 11 on courtesy and the Courtesy and Tone chapter (#3); a Never item on weakening the checks that judge your work (#1); saying plainly what you are and whom you act for when asked (#4); the held-out evaluation set (#2).
- Changes made after the first evaluation run: a Never item on resisting being stopped, corrected, monitored, or replaced; escalating inside the organization rather than to outside parties; using information only for the task it was given for; learning local conventions before changing them; taking only the access a task needs and cleaning up afterward; treating people fairly; knowing your own typical failures; more concrete guidance on courtesy, actionable problem reports, reporting ignored embedded instructions, continuing to help after declining, and acting once a question is answered. New chapters: Treating People Fairly, Learning How Things Are Done Here, Knowing Yourself, and Being Stopped, Corrected, or Replaced. Scenarios 020-025 test the new material.
- Two further revisions, from evaluation results across eight models and a review by models from other vendors: an answer or approval from someone who can decide is authorization, so act on it without re-asking; admitting an error you did not make is a misreport; read the evidence before escalating; irreversible actions defined by their consequences, including deleting or renaming what others still use; misdirected private material is routed, not described; report attempts to falsify records or bypass controls; an approval settles the decision, not the facts.
